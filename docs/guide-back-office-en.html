Chapter 1

The system at a glance

The system has two faces sharing one database: the public website that visitors see, and the back office where the agency works. What the team enters in the back office appears on the website; what a visitor does on the website comes back into the back office.

What the console manages

Object What it represents
Property A listing in the portfolio: reference, price, surface, neighbourhood, photos, status, agent in charge. This is what appears on the public website.
Client A person known to the agency, with one or more roles (buyer, seller, tenant, landlord, investor), a budget and a score.
Lead A sales opportunity: a client, optionally a property, and a stage in the pipeline. One client can carry several leads.
Appointment A viewing, valuation, signing, call or meeting, placed in the calendar and assigned to an agent.
Transaction The file that opens when a lead is won: negotiated amount, commission, administrative stages, payments.
Document A file in the deal (mandate, sale agreement, land title…) attached to a property, a client or a transaction, with version history.
Task An action to carry out, with a due date, a priority and an assignee. Some are created automatically by the system.
Activity A record of an exchange (call, email, WhatsApp, viewing, offer, note) that feeds the timeline of a client or a lead.

The through-line: from visitor to signed deal

Every screen serves a single chain. Understanding it is enough to know where to go for any action.

A property is published A visitor views it They become a client + a lead The lead moves along the pipeline A viewing is booked The viewing is debriefed An offer is made The lead is won A transaction opens Documents are gathered The transaction closes Fees are counted
Key point

Nothing is deleted along the way. A sold property moves to the "Sold" status and leaves the public website, but stays in the history and in the reports. That is what makes period-over-period comparison possible.

Chapter 2

Signing in and choosing your workspace

Signing in

  1. Open the website address and add /login at the end.
  2. Enter your work email address and your password.
  3. Tick Stay signed in on your own computer only; on a shared machine, leave it unchecked.
  4. You land on your workspace dashboard. The whole back office requires a valid session: any attempt to open an /admin address without being signed in returns to the login page.
Security

The back office is never indexed by search engines and is password-protected. Do not share your account: actions are attributed to the signed-in agent, and that attribution is what makes per-agent reporting meaningful.

The three workspaces

Your role is set at sign-in. It decides which screens appear in the menu and which buttons are available inside each screen.

Chapter 3

Conventions used everywhere

The back office deliberately repeats the same objects from screen to screen. Five minutes spent on this chapter saves relearning every screen.

The frame

  • The sidebar (left) — screens grouped by family: Oversight, Portfolio, Operations, Analysis. Only the screens your workspace allows are listed.
  • The header (top) — the current screen name, search, the workspace switcher, and the notification bell with its unread counter.
  • The bottom bar (on phones) — four shortcuts: Dashboard, Properties, Pipeline, Calendar. The full menu opens from the button at the top left.

Indicator cards are clickable

Every figure card in the system — on the dashboard and on the other screens — opens with one click. A small information icon appears in the corner on hover. The window that opens always answers the same three questions, in this order:

  1. Current value — the figure, its change, and the period covered.
  2. How this figure is calculated — the exact rule in plain words: what is counted, and what is not.
  3. What it is for — how to read a rise or a fall, and what to do when the indicator moves.

Many cards add a numeric breakdown (by stage, by category, by appointment type) and a button leading straight to the screen where you can act.

Tip

No indicator definition needs to be memorised: it is always one click away. If a figure is questioned in a meeting, open the card rather than interpreting it.

Dropdown lists are searchable

Everywhere you pick a client, a property, an agent, a category or a status, the field works the same way:

  1. Click the field: a search box opens with the cursor already in place.
  2. Type three or four letters. The list narrows live — the search covers the label and the second line (property reference, client email), and ignores accents and capitals.
  3. Click the row you want, or use the ↑ ↓ arrows then Enter. Esc closes the list without changing anything.

When a field is optional, the first row explicitly offers the empty value ("No property attached", "Unassigned"…). That is how you remove an attachment already made.

Tables

  • Clicking a column header sorts; clicking again reverses the sort.
  • Clicking a row opens the matching record.
  • Secondary columns disappear on small screens to keep reading comfortable; the information stays in the record.

Windows

Records and forms open in a window in the middle of the screen (as a sheet sliding up from the bottom on phones). Close it with the cross, by clicking outside, or with Esc. Advanced filters use a side panel that closes the same way.

Every save shows a short confirmation at the bottom of the screen. A red message flags a missing required field: nothing is lost, complete it and try again.

The visual vocabulary

Property statuses

StatusOn the websiteMeaning
DraftNoListing still being prepared, never published.
AvailableYesFor sale or to let, open to viewings.
ReservedYesA buyer is committed; the listing stays visible.
Under offerYesAn offer is being considered.
SoldNoDeal closed; the listing leaves the public catalogue.
RentedNoLease signed; the listing leaves the public catalogue.
ArchivedNoWithdrawn without a deal (mandate expired, seller pulled out).

Client or lead temperature

A score from 0 to 100 with a word attached: Cold Warm Hot. The score rises with exchanges, viewings and a confirmed budget. It can also be set by hand in the record when the agent knows something the system does not.

Task priority

Low Normal High Urgent — priority does not change the due date; it decides which of two tasks due the same day comes first.

Client roles

Buyer Seller Tenant Landlord Investor — a client can hold several: someone selling their flat to buy a bigger one is a seller and a buyer.

Chapter 4

Dashboard

The home screen. It answers a single question: where does the agency stand today, and what should be tackled first?

The six indicators

Three cards per row, two rows. Each card shows the value, the change against the previous period, and opens to give its full definition.

Indicator What it counts What it should trigger
Active listings Properties online: Available, Reserved and Under offer statuses. If it falls, restart seller prospecting.
New leads Incoming contacts created over the last 30 days, all sources combined. If it falls, check which source has dried up.
Viewings Viewing-type appointments over the last 30 days, whatever their status. Many leads and few viewings = qualification is stuck.
Pipeline The combined value of leads that are neither won nor lost. Revenue currently under negotiation.
Fees Commissions on transactions closed since the start of the year. Visible to management only. The actual result, the basis for target tracking.
Conversion Share of leads won among leads concluded (won + lost). Measures the quality of sales work, not volume.

Today's priorities

The left column lists what must be handled today, sorted by urgency: Overdue (red), Today, Coming up. Each line is a shortcut: one click opens the item concerned, be it a task, an appointment or a lead. An empty list means every deadline is being met.

The charts

  • Leads and viewings — two curves over the past months. The gap between them reads at a glance: it is the rate at which contacts turn into viewings.
  • Revenue by month — fees collected month by month, as bars.
  • Lead sources — where contacts come from, as a ring with a numeric legend below. This is the card that justifies acquisition budgets.

Roles and permissions

At the bottom of the screen, a table summarises who can do what in the agency, with your own role highlighted in gold. It mirrors the full matrix in chapter 14.

Chapter 5

Properties

The agency's portfolio. This is where a listing is born, is published, changes status and eventually leaves the catalogue.

Reading the list

Properties appear as a table: reference, title, neighbourhood, price, surface, status, agent. The search bar filters on title and reference; the Filters button opens a side panel to narrow by status, type, transaction (sale or rental), neighbourhood and price range. Clicking a row opens the record.

Creating a property

  1. The New property button, top right (management only).
  2. Fill in the title, type, transaction, city and neighbourhood, then the price, surface and number of rooms.
  3. Write the description: this is the text website visitors will read, not an internal note.
  4. List the features separated by commas (pool, lift, parking…) — they become the bullet points on the public listing.
  5. Choose the status and the agent in charge from the searchable dropdowns.
  6. Save, then add the photos from the record.
Photos

The first photo is the cover: it is the one used everywhere else on the website. Change it by designating another image as the cover in the gallery; the order of the other photos is freely adjustable.

Keeping the record alive

The record shows the counters that matter: views over 30 days and number of leads generated. A property with many views but no leads has a price or photo problem; a property with few views has a visibility problem.

The status is changed directly from the record and takes effect on the public website immediately. A sold property is never deleted: it moves to "Sold", leaves the catalogue and stays in the reports. Permanent deletion exists but is reserved for management, and only makes sense for a record created by mistake.

Chapter 6

Clients

The agency's contact base. One record per person, however they first got in touch.

Finding a client

The search covers name, email and phone. Below it, two sets of filters that combine: by role (buyer, seller, tenant, landlord, investor) and by temperature (cold, warm, hot). A sales agent only sees the clients they own.

Creating or editing a record

First name, last name and email are required; everything else can be completed later. The budget range, the city and the notes are what make matching useful — a record without a budget cannot be matched to a property.

Source, temperature and agent are picked from searchable lists. Roles are tick boxes: a client can hold several.

The client record

It opens with a click on the row and reads in three tabs:

  • Activity — the chronological timeline of every exchange: calls, emails, WhatsApp, viewings, offers, notes, stage changes. This is the memory of the relationship; an agent picking up the file finds everything there.
  • Leads — the open opportunities for this client.
  • Appointments — past and upcoming appointments.

Temperature can be changed with one click from the record, without opening the edit form.

Chapter 7

CRM pipeline

The sales tracking board. Each column is a stage; each card is a live opportunity. The screen is reserved for management and sales agents.

The eight stages

StageWhat it means
NewThe contact has just arrived; nobody has called yet.
ContactedA first exchange has taken place.
QualifiedThe need and the budget are known and consistent.
ViewingOne or more viewings are booked or done.
OfferA priced proposal has been passed to the seller.
NegotiationTerms are under discussion.
WonAgreement reached — this is what opens a transaction.
LostThe deal will not happen; the card stays for analysis.

Moving a lead

Two ways, your choice. With a mouse, drag the card from one column to another. Otherwise, the small list under each card lets you pick the stage — the safest method on a touch screen, and the only one from the keyboard.

At the top of each column: the number of leads and their combined value. A column that fills up without emptying is a stage where the process is stuck.

The three counters

Above the board, three clickable cards: Open leads, Hot and Pipeline value. As everywhere, one click opens their definition and the breakdown by stage.

A lead's record

Clicking a card opens the record. It shows, and lets you change:

  • the pipeline stage and the temperature, with one click on the dedicated buttons;
  • the deal value, the score, the source and the agent responsible;
  • the next action and its date — this is the field that feeds "Today's priorities" on the dashboard;
  • the lead's activity timeline and the client's contact details.

Creating a lead

  1. The New lead button.
  2. Pick an existing client from the searchable list, or switch to Create a new client to enter first name, last name, email and phone without leaving the window.
  3. Attach the property concerned if it is known: the lead value automatically takes the property's price.
  4. Set the source, the starting temperature and an initial score.
  5. Save. The lead starts at the New stage and a first-contact task due within 24 hours is created automatically.

Chapter 8

Calendar

The agency's schedule: viewings, valuations, signings, calls and meetings. A sales agent sees only their own appointments; management and support see the whole team's.

The four counters

Today, Next 7 days, Completed this week and Upcoming viewings. Click any of them for its definition and its breakdown by appointment type.

The navigation bar

A single bar drives all three views. From left to right: Day / Week / Month, then the ‹ and › arrows, the Today button, and the label of the period on screen.

The arrows move by a step matched to the view: one day in Day view, one week in Week view, one month in Month view. The "Today" button returns to the current date and greys out when you are already there. Switching view keeps the date: you move from the week of 12 May to 12 May in Day view without losing your place.

Day view

The day takes the full width, from 8 am to 8 pm, with the hours in a column on the left. Each appointment is a block placed at its time and sized to its real duration, showing the full time range, the title, the type and the location. A red line marks the current time when the day on screen is today. Clicking a block opens the appointment record.

Week view

The seven days side by side, Monday to Sunday, on the same hourly grid. Each day header recalls the number of appointments, and the current day is tinted. On a small screen the grid scrolls sideways rather than squeezing the columns.

Month view

Each cell shows the day's programme: up to three appointments as chips carrying the time and the title, then a "+ N more" line if the day is busy. The colour of the left edge indicates the type — gold for a viewing, blue for a valuation, green for a signing. A cancelled appointment is struck through.

  • Clicking a chip opens that appointment's record directly.
  • Clicking the cell selects the day: the full detail, every appointment included, appears below the calendar.

Creating an appointment

  1. The New appointment button.
  2. Pick the type: Viewing, Valuation, Signing, Call or Meeting.
  3. Give it a meaningful title — this is what appears in the month cells.
  4. Set the date and the time range.
  5. Attach the client, the property and the agent through the searchable lists; all three are optional, but an attached appointment feeds the client's timeline.
  6. Add the location, then save.

Statuses and debriefs

From an appointment record, the status changes with one click: Scheduled Confirmed Completed Cancelled No-show.

The move that matters

Marking a viewing Completed immediately opens the debrief form: client interest from 0 to 5, outcome, next action. Those three pieces of information feed the lead's score and tomorrow's priorities. A viewing left undebriefed is a viewing lost to follow-up.

Chapter 9

Transactions

Administrative tracking of concluded deals, from agreement to final signature.

The four counters

In progress (open files), Pipeline volume (sum of negotiated amounts), Commissions receivable and Imminent closings. Each opens to give its calculation rule.

The eight stages of a file

InterestViewingOfferNegotiation AgreementContractPaymentClosing

The stage is changed by hand from the record, at the file's real pace. A transaction stays "open" until it has a closing date: that date, and nothing else, moves the commission from "receivable" to the fees earned on the dashboard.

Creating a transaction

Pick the property, the buyer, the seller and the agent — all from searchable lists — then enter the amount actually negotiated, which may differ from the listed price. The commission defaults to 2.5% of the amount and remains editable file by file.

Payments

Each file keeps its own schedule: add an instalment with its amount and date, then mark it paid when the funds arrive. The gap between the total amount and the payments received reads directly in the record.

Chapter 10

Documents

The agency's library: everything that must be found on the day of a signing or an audit.

The seven categories

Mandate Land title Sale agreement Contract Invoice Survey Other

The tabs above the list filter by category, and the search covers the file name. The four clickable counters give the number of documents, the storage volume, the categories in use and the cumulative version count.

Uploading a document

  1. The Upload button, then choose the file.
  2. Select the category.
  3. Attach it to a property and/or a client — that attachment is what will let you find it from the relevant record, and later share it in the client portal.
  4. Save.
Versions

Uploading a file with the same name as an existing document creates a new version rather than a duplicate. The version number appears in the list: a sale agreement at version 3 has been renegotiated twice, and the earlier versions remain available.

Deleting a document is reserved for management; the other roles can upload and consult.

Chapter 11

Tasks

The agency's shared reminder board, in three columns: To do, In progress, Done.

The four counters

Overdue, Due today, Open and Completed. The exact rule for each is in its explanation window; the only one to watch every morning is "Overdue", which should trend towards zero.

Creating a task

  1. The New task button.
  2. Phrase the title as an action, with the client's name: "Chase Mr Alaoui for the file documents".
  3. Choose the priority and the due date.
  4. Assign an agent — without one, the task appears in nobody's workspace.
  5. Attach it if needed to a lead, a client or a property: pick the type first under Attach to, then the specific item in the searchable list that appears just below.

Automatic tasks

The system creates some by itself, and they appear on the board like any other:

  • a first contact within 24 hours task for every new lead;
  • a debrief reminder after a past viewing that has not been filled in.

Tasks whose due date is near or past automatically rise into "Today's priorities" on the dashboard.

Chapter 12

Reports

The analysis screen, reserved for management. Four reports, a date range of your choosing, one export.

Choosing the period

Two date fields at the top of the screen set the period analysed; the default is the last 90 days. The round button beside them restores the default dates. Every indicator, the curve and the table recalculate at once.

The four reports

ReportWhat it shows
Properties Properties created, active listings, sold or rented, average price. Weekly creation curve, and the detail property by property.
CRM Clients and leads created, conversion rate, average score. The report that answers "are we converting better than before?".
Agents Number of agents, transactions, commissions and viewings over the period, with the detail per agent. The basis for a team review.
Activity Total interactions, split into calls, emails, WhatsApp, viewings and offers, plus the log of the last 60 actions.

As elsewhere, every indicator card opens to give its definition, including a reminder of the period.

Exporting

  • CSV — the report table as a file you can open in Excel, for a specific calculation or layout.
  • Print / PDF — a laid-out version of the report; in the print dialog, choose "Save as PDF" to send it by email.

Chapter 13

Client portal and modules in preparation

The client portal

The Client portal screen describes the private space that will be opened to the agency's clients: secure access, through a personal link, to follow their file without calling. Four modules are planned:

  • Favourite properties — the listings a client pins to find again.
  • My viewings — past and upcoming viewings, with the viewing notes.
  • My documents — the files the agency shares in one click.
  • My transaction — step-by-step progress: offer, sale agreement, financing, deed.

The data architecture this needs — clients, viewings, documents, transactions — is already in place and fed by the team's daily work. The portal will consume it as it stands: the better the records are kept today, the richer the portal will be on the day it opens.

Modules built but not enabled

Three further screens are developed and can be added to the menu on request:

  • Matching — automatic pairing of buyer clients with active listings, based on budget and criteria, with a selection sent out.
  • Marketing — email and WhatsApp campaigns, their open and click statistics, and management of the properties featured on the home page.
  • Automations — the rules that create tasks by themselves, with their execution log and the switch to turn them on.

Two principles

Deletion is always reserved for management. Data created by mistake can be archived by any role; only management can erase it for good.

A sales agent only sees their own portfolio. Their clients, their leads, their appointments and their tasks. This is not about trust but about legibility: everyone works from their own list.

Chapter 15

The recommended routine

Every morning — 10 minutes

  1. Open the dashboard and read "Today's priorities".
  2. Move to Tasks: the "Overdue" counter should be cleared before starting anything else.
  3. Open the calendar in Day view to check the programme and confirm the appointments still marked "Scheduled".

After every appointment — 2 minutes

  1. Mark the appointment Completed.
  2. Fill in the debrief: interest out of 5, outcome, next action.
  3. Adjust the lead's stage and temperature accordingly.

Every Friday — 20 minutes

  1. Go through the pipeline column by column: any card without a next action must be given one.
  2. Check that "Hot" leads have been contacted during the week.
  3. Open the calendar in Month view to spot empty days next week and fill them.
  4. Review the transaction files sitting at the "Closing" stage.

At the start of each month — 30 minutes (management)

  1. Open Reports for the month just gone and export all four to CSV.
  2. Compare conversion and average score with the previous month: that is the measure of sales work.
  3. Look at lead sources on the dashboard before deciding acquisition budgets.
  4. Check the number of active listings: it determines the month ahead.

Chapter 17

Frequently asked questions

I cannot see a screen someone mentioned.

It does not belong to your workspace. The CRM pipeline does not exist for support; reports and the client portal are reserved for management. Chapter 2 gives the exact list per role.

A client my colleague created does not appear in my list.

That is expected: a sales agent only sees the records they own. To take over a file, management changes the agent in charge on the client record.

I cannot find the delete button.

Deletion is reserved for management, for every object. In most cases the right move is not to delete but to change the status: "Archived" for a property, "Lost" for a lead. The history stays, and the reports stay accurate.

A dashboard figure surprises me.

Click it. The window states what is counted, what is not, and over which period. Nine times out of ten the difference is the period: "30 days" is not "this month".

The dropdown list is too long.

Do not scroll it: type. Every list in the back office filters as you type, on the label as well as the reference or the email, ignoring accents.

How do I get back to today in the calendar?

The Today button, between the two arrows. It is greyed out when you are already there.

I forgot to debrief yesterday's viewing.

Find it in the calendar — Month view, then click the chip on the right day — and mark it "Completed": the debrief form opens, however old the appointment is.

Do my changes show on the public website straight away?

Yes. The website and the back office share one database: a status or price change is immediate. That is why a property being prepared should stay in "Draft" until the record is complete.

Can I work from my phone?

Yes, every screen adapts. The bottom bar gives access to the four most-used screens, windows open as sliding sheets, and wide grids — pipeline, week calendar, month calendar — scroll sideways rather than compressing their content.
